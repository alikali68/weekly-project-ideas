# Briefly — AI-Powered Client Brief Builder for Freelancers

**One-line pitch:** Turn messy client calls and scattered DMs into a structured, sign-off-ready project brief in minutes — freelancers send one smart questionnaire link, Briefly turns the answers into scope, timeline, and a quote.

## Problem & target users

Freelancers lose hours (and money) to unclear briefs. A client says "I need a website" in a WhatsApp voice note, the freelancer guesses the scope, quotes too low, and the project drifts into unpaid revisions. The brief — the single document that should prevent all of this — is usually a Google Doc nobody fills in properly.

**Target users:**
- Solo freelance designers, developers, and copywriters who take on 2–8 client projects a month
- Small studios (2–10 people) that onboard clients without a project manager
- Clients themselves, who get a guided, jargon-free questionnaire instead of a blank document

## Tech stack

- **Next.js 15 (App Router, TypeScript)** — one codebase for the marketing site, the questionnaire flow, and the dashboard; server components keep the brief pages fast and SEO-friendly.
- **Tailwind CSS + shadcn/ui** — a clean, professional questionnaire UI that looks trustworthy to non-technical clients, built from accessible primitives.
- **PostgreSQL + Prisma** — relational data (briefs, questions, answers, quotes) fits a typed ORM; migrations keep the schema honest as the product grows.
- **Clerk** — auth for freelancers with social login and organizations, so a studio can share briefs without building auth from scratch.
- **OpenAI API (GPT-4o-mini)** — turns raw questionnaire answers into a structured brief draft, a scope list, and a risk flag ("client didn't specify a budget — ask before quoting"); the cheap model keeps per-brief cost under a cent.
- **Resend** — transactional email: "your brief is ready", client reminders, and PDF delivery.
- **Stripe** — subscriptions and one-off brief-pack purchases.
- **Vercel** — hosting with preview deployments; the questionnaire must load instantly on a client's phone.

## Architecture overview

```
Client (questionnaire link) ─┐
                             ├─► Next.js App Router ─► Prisma ─► PostgreSQL
Freelancer (dashboard) ──────┘        │
                                      ├─► OpenAI API (brief generation)
                                      ├─► Clerk (auth)
                                      ├─► Resend (email)
                                      └─► Stripe (billing)
```

- **Public questionnaire route** (`/b/<token>`): no login required for the client; a signed, expiring token identifies the brief. Answers autosave as the client types.
- **Brief engine**: a server action takes the raw answers, runs a structured-output prompt, and stores the generated brief as a versioned draft — the freelancer always edits before sending, so the AI never publishes unchecked.
- **PDF export**: the approved brief renders to a print-ready PDF (Puppeteer on a serverless function) with the freelancer's branding, ready to attach to a contract.
- **Quote calculator**: the freelancer sets hourly/day rates once; the brief's scope items map to estimated hours, producing a quote range the freelancer can adjust.

## Data model

- **User** — id, name, email, avatarUrl, businessName, logoUrl, hourlyRate, currency, plan (free/pro), createdAt
- **Template** — id, userId, name, category (website | branding | webapp | custom), isPublic, createdAt
- **Question** — id, templateId, order, type (short_text | long_text | single_choice | multi_choice | budget_range | date), label, helpText, options (JSON), required
- **Brief** — id, userId, templateId, clientName, clientEmail, status (draft | awaiting_client | in_review | approved | archived), shareToken, tokenExpiresAt, aiSummary, createdAt, updatedAt
- **Answer** — id, briefId, questionId, value (JSON), updatedAt
- **BriefVersion** — id, briefId, versionNumber, content (JSON: goals, audience, scope[], deliverables[], timeline, budget, risks[]), generatedBy (ai | human), createdAt
- **Quote** — id, briefId, lineItems (JSON: {label, hours, rate}), totalLow, totalHigh, currency, status (draft | sent | accepted)

## Seed / initial data

**Template: "Website Design & Build"** (starter, public)
- Q1 — "What is the main goal of the website?" (long_text, required) — help: "e.g. sell products, get leads, show a portfolio"
- Q2 — "Who is the website for?" (long_text, required)
- Q3 — "Which pages do you need?" (multi_choice: Home, About, Services, Shop, Blog, Contact, Booking)
- Q4 — "Do you have a logo and brand colors?" (single_choice: Yes — I'll share them / No — I need branding too / Partially)
- Q5 — "What is your budget range?" (budget_range: < $500 / $500–1,500 / $1,500–5,000 / $5,000+)
- Q6 — "When do you need it live?" (date)

**Sample brief (seed):** "Website for Bloom Bakery — client Sara Ahmadi"
- Goals: take online orders for custom cakes; show the weekly menu
- Audience: local customers, 25–45, ordering for events
- Scope: Home, Menu, Order form, Contact; 6 pages total
- Budget: $1,500–5,000 · Deadline: 2026-12-20
- Risks flagged by AI: no logo supplied; photography not mentioned — ask before quoting

**Sample quote:** 6 pages × ~6h design/build = 36h × $25/h → **$900–$1,200**

## Build roadmap

- **Week 1 — Foundation:** Next.js scaffold, Clerk auth, Prisma schema + Postgres, freelancer profile CRUD, template builder UI with the 3 starter templates.
- **Week 2 — Questionnaire:** public `/b/<token>` flow, autosave answers, progress bar, mobile-first design, email invite to client.
- **Week 3 — AI brief engine:** OpenAI structured-output prompt, brief versioning, editor UI, approve/send flow, client sign-off.
- **Week 4 — Polish & launch:** PDF export, quote calculator, Resend emails, Stripe Pro plan, landing page, deploy to Vercel, launch on X + Product Hunt.

## Monetization angle

Freemium: free plan covers 3 active briefs a month with Briefly branding on the PDF; Pro at $12/month removes the cap and branding, adds the quote calculator and reminders. A $49 one-off "Brief Pack" (10 briefs, no subscription) catches freelancers who only take occasional projects. The real wedge is trust: a freelancer who sends a polished, AI-structured brief wins the client before the work starts — and every brief a client signs off on is a Briefly-branded touchpoint in front of the next potential customer.
